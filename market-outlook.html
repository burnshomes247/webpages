Things to Watch
  • Federal workforce reductions (DOGE)
    The DC metro is uniquely exposed to federal employment. Sustained layoffs could dampen demand in close-in VA/MD suburbs. Scale and pace of cuts remain the key variable.
  • Tariff-driven construction cost inflation
    New tariffs on lumber, steel, and appliances are slowing new construction starts, which supports existing home prices — but also raises renovation costs for sellers preparing to list.
  • Fed policy trajectory
    The Fed has signaled caution on rate cuts in 2025. Timing of any cuts will directly affect buyer purchasing power and demand intensity.
  • Inventory remains historically low
    Fewer competing listings means your home commands more attention and stronger offers. Sellers are still operating in a supply-constrained environment.
  • Prices are near cyclical highs
    NoVA and close-in DC suburbs are up ~4% YoY. Waiting for a higher price risks giving back gains if rates drop and inventory surges simultaneously.
  • Motivated, qualified buyers in the market
    Buyers who have been waiting are ready to act quickly on well-priced homes. Multiple-offer situations are still common in desirable submarkets.
  • Federal workforce uncertainty creating urgency
    DOGE and federal agency restructuring are prompting some government employees to sell before potential relocation or income disruption. This creates near-term motivated buyer demand from peers also repositioning.
    Watch list: could flip to headwind if job losses accelerate
  • Rate relief could expand the buyer pool significantly
    If 30-year rates drop from ~6.8% to the mid-5s, millions of sidelined buyers re-enter the market. A seller who waits could benefit from a larger, more competitive buyer pool.
  • Pre-sale improvements could increase net proceeds
    Strategic updates — fresh paint, kitchen refresh, landscaping — can yield 3–5x ROI. A seller with time to prepare may net more than one who lists quickly.
  • Spring 2026 inventory spike not yet materialized
    If new construction deliveries or job-loss listings flood the market, competition increases. Waiting to gauge inventory levels in Q1 may be prudent.
  • 10-Year Treasury Bond
    The 10-year Treasury yield has been increasing, and it's the benchmark mortgage rates are priced off of — when it rises, mortgage rates tend to follow. It needs to come down for rates, and the broader market, to find real stability.
